01

One production queue. Ninety days.

The pilot is a live gate on one production queue — the queue that already writes, pays, deletes, notifies, or changes state. You keep the agent runtime. Latch owns the decision and the record.

04

VPC and security questionnaire

Answers a security team can take into review. Architecture diagrams and the current questionnaire ride with the pilot PO — not a public certification claim.

Where does Latch run?

In your VPC. Policy, gate API, audit store, and escalation stay on your side of the boundary. Latch is not a cloud SKU that has to see the traffic first.

What does the runtime send?

The action proposal: tool name, typed arguments, actor, and policy context. Full prompt corpora are out unless you explicitly configure that field in.

What is written to audit?

Decision (approve | deny | escalate), confidence, policy version, actor, action digest, and timestamp. Records are append-only. They are not edited in place.

How does redaction work?

You define the redaction path before anything is stored. Secrets, raw document bodies, and fields you mark never enter the audit payload. If a field cannot be redacted, it does not get logged.

How is the runtime authenticated?

Workload identity: mTLS or the identity plane you already use. Latch authenticates the caller, not an end-user directory we host.

Does Latch require a hosted model?

No. High-confidence decisions skip the model. Escalation may call a model you already run, or a named human. Latch does not require a Latch-hosted LLM.

What is excluded from the latency SLA?

Your network, DNS, and the runtime’s own queueing. p95 ≤ 500ms is Latch ingress to typed response.

Do you claim a public certification?

No. We send the questionnaire, data-flow diagram, and the VPC deployment notes. Ask for those; do not infer a certificate from this page.
